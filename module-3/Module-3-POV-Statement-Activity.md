# POV Statement Activity

Your POV Statement is the foundation of your entire Define Phase. It crystallizes your self-knowledge into a single, honest, actionable articulation of your academic direction. This is not a formal mission statement — it is a working tool. It will evolve. But you need a starting point, and this activity helps you build one.

---

## Step 1 — Review Your Module 2 Work (10–15 minutes)

Open your previous assignments and read through your key insights from:

- Your SWOT Matrix (especially Strengths and Opportunities)
- Empathy Map

As you read, highlight or note the three to five themes that appear most consistently. You are looking for patterns — things that showed up in multiple places.

**Themes I noticed:**
1. I overthink a lot.
2. I have lots of motivation.
3. I FEEL like I am very behind.
4. Failure slows me down.
5. I have very strong goals for my future.

---

## Step 2 — Answer the POV Builder Questions

Write honest answers to each of the following questions. Do not overthink — write your first genuine response.

**1. Who am I in this moment, academically and personally?**
*(Consider: your background, your identity, your situation as a college student)*
I am a college student, but I am also someone who has experienced a lot of change and different environments throughout my life. I have lived in India, Florida, North Carolina, two different cities in Massachusetts, and Tennessee. Moving so much has allowed me to experience different places, people, and ways of life and has helped me become adaptable and mature for my age. Even though I have become comfortable with change, uncertainty can still scare me, especially when I don't know what is going to happen next. Academically, I am still figuring out exactly what direction I want my future to take, but I know I have strong goals and want to build a meaningful and successful life.

**2. What do I genuinely need from my college education?**
*(Consider: what would make this experience feel meaningful — not just successful)*
I need my college education to give me the opportunity to explore different interests and figure out what I truly want to do with my future. I don't just want a degree; I want to gain experiences, skills, and knowledge that I can actually use in the real world. I want my education to help me become more confident in my decisions and prepare me for a career that is both financially successful and meaningful to me. I also want to learn how to deal with uncertainty instead of letting it stop me from trying new things.

**3. What is the most important thing I learned about myself in Module 2?**
*(Consider: something surprising, clarifying, or that shifted how you see yourself)*
The biggest thing I learned about myself is that even though I tend to overthink and worry about failure, I have already handled a lot of change in my life. Moving from India to different states and cities taught me that change can be scary but can also lead to something better. I realized that I am more adaptable than I sometimes give myself credit for. I also learned that I have very strong goals, and even when I feel behind, I still have a lot of motivation to create the future I want.

**4. What kind of work energizes me? What drains me?**
*(Consider: from your Empathy Map and Ikigai reflection)*
I am energized by work that allows me to explore new ideas, solve problems, be creative, and make an impact. I am especially interested in technology and AI because I think they have the potential to solve real-world problems. I want to do work where I can continue learning instead of doing the exact same thing every day. Work that feels meaningless, repetitive, or has no connection to a bigger purpose drains me. I also struggle when I put too much pressure on myself to make the perfect decision or become afraid of failing.

**5. What problem or challenge do I most want to contribute to solving in the world?**
*(Consider: even a vague answer is useful here)*
Two problems that stand out to me are world hunger and homelessness. Seeing how many people struggle to meet basic needs makes me want to eventually contribute to solutions that can improve people's lives. I am interested in finding ways that technology, business, and innovation could potentially help address these problems on a larger scale. I don't know exactly what my solution will look like yet, but I know I want my future work to contribute to something bigger than myself.

## Step 3 — Draft Your POV Statement

Use the following template to draft your POV Statement. Write it in your own voice — not in formal or academic language. Write **at least two drafts** of your POV Statement and choose the one that feels most honest and most useful.

> "I am [brief description of who you are — background, identity, situation]. I need [a genuine academic or career need you identified]. Because [the most important insight from your self-assessment work]."

**Example:**
> "I am a first-generation college student from a small town who has always been drawn to science but also deeply values connection to people and community. I need a degree path that lets me combine analytical thinking with direct human impact, because I have learned that I lose motivation and meaning when my work feels disconnected from real people's lives."

### Draft 1
I am a college student who has lived in many different places and experienced a lot of change throughout my life, which has taught me to adapt and become mature for my age. I need a college education that gives me the freedom to explore my interests, develop useful skills, and prepare for a successful career that has meaning. Because I have learned that change can be scary but can also lead to something better, I want to stop letting uncertainty and fear of failure keep me from taking chances and discovering what I am capable of.

### Draft 2
I am an ambitious and adaptable person whose experiences moving between India and different parts of the United States have shaped the way I see the world. I need an education that allows me to combine my interest in technology and AI with my desire to solve real problems, while also giving me the confidence to navigate an uncertain future. Because I have learned through my experiences that I can adapt to change even when it scares me, I want to use that strength to take more risks, learn from failure, and eventually contribute to problems like hunger and homelessness.

### Final POV Statement
*(Choose the draft — or a refined combination — that feels most honest and most useful.)*

I am an ambitious and adaptable college student whose experiences living in India and different parts of the United States have taught me how much change can shape a person. I need a college education that allows me to explore my interests in technology and AI, develop skills for a successful career, and eventually use what I learn to help solve real-world problems like hunger and homelessness. Because I have learned that change and uncertainty can be scary but can also lead to something better, I need to trust myself more, take chances, and not let overthinking or fear of failure keep me from moving toward the future I want.

## Step 4 — Reflect

Below your final POV Statement, write a short paragraph (5–8 sentences) responding to the following:

- What was difficult about writing your POV Statement?
- What did you discover or clarify in the process?
- Does your POV Statement surprise you in any way?
- How does it connect to your Module 2 findings?

**My Reflection:**
Writing my POV Statement was difficult because I realized that I have many different interests and goals, so it's hard to put everything into one direction. I also realized that a lot of my personality has been shaped by how much I have moved throughout my life. Living in India, Florida, North Carolina, Massachusetts, and Tennessee exposed me to different environments and taught me how to adapt to change. Even though I have become used to change, I still get scared when I don't know what is going to happen next. My POV Statement helped me realize that uncertainty is not necessarily a bad thing because some of the biggest changes in my life have also brought good experiences. It also connects to my Module 2 findings because I tend to overthink, fear failure, and feel behind, even though I have already shown that I can adapt to difficult changes. I realized that I don't need to have my entire future figured out right now; I need to keep exploring and trust myself enough to move forward.
