# Career-to-Degree Map Activity

This activity bridges your career interests and your academic options. You will research two specific career paths, identify what they require, and map those requirements back onto potential degree combinations.

---

## Step 1 — Identify 2 Career Paths to Research

Based on your Ikigai reflection and your POV Statement, identify two specific career roles — not just broad fields, but actual job titles or professional roles. For example, not "healthcare" but "health policy analyst" or "clinical social worker."

An AI Product Manager is interesting because current job postings emphasize AI knowledge, product strategy, analytics, communication, stakeholder management, and working with technical teams, not just writing code. ## Step 2 — Research Each Career Path

For each career, use O*NET, the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, LinkedIn, and any other credible source to gather the following:

### Career 1

**Career Title:**
AI Product Manager

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
An AI Product Manager helps plan, develop, and improve products that use artificial intelligence. Their day-to-day work can include identifying customer needs, creating product roadmaps, defining requirements, working with engineers and data teams, analyzing product performance, communicating with stakeholders, and deciding how AI features should be developed and improved. Current AI product-management postings emphasize AI/ML knowledge, roadmap planning, analytics, user research, communication, and collaboration with technical teams.


**Typical Education Required:**
A bachelor's degree in a relevant field such as Computer Science, Data Science, Engineering, Business, or a related field is commonly requested for AI product-management roles. A current LinkedIn AI Product Manager posting lists a bachelor's degree or equivalent practical experience and emphasizes AI/ML knowledge and product-management skills.


**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Communication
2. Product management
3. Critical thinking
4. Data analysis
5. Leadership
6. Problem solving
7. AI/ML knowledge
8. Project management

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Artificial intelligence and machine learning, product development, data analysis, user needs, technology, business strategy, and project management.

**Median Annual Salary:**
There is not a separate BLS occupational category for "AI Product Manager," so salary varies depending on the employer, industry, and level of experience. AI product managers are generally considered part of the broader product-management/technology-management workforce.

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing. Demand for AI products and technology is increasing, and organizations are continuing to expand their use of artificial intelligence. BLS projects 16% growth from 2025–2035 for the closely related occupation of computer and information systems managers, compared with 3% for all occupations.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
A person can begin with a bachelor's degree and gain experience through internships, technology projects, business analysis, software/technology roles, or associate product-management positions. They can then advance into Product Manager, Senior Product Manager, Group Product Manager, and eventually Director or Vice President of Product roles.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
AI 101/AI 102 → Build foundational AI and technology knowledge.
CECS 250 → Develop knowledge of data science, computational tools, and applied AI.
AI-related internship or product project → Develop real-world product, communication, and project-management experience.

### Career 2

**Career Title:**
Computer Systems Analyst

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
Computer Systems Analysts evaluate an organization's computer systems and technology needs and help determine how technology can improve business operations. Their work can include analyzing problems, communicating with users, evaluating systems, testing technology, recommending improvements, and helping organizations implement new systems. O*NET identifies systems analysis, systems evaluation, critical thinking, communication, active listening, and complex problem solving as important skills for this occupation.

**Typical Education Required:**
A bachelor's degree is commonly associated with this occupation, although O*NET indicates that education requirements can vary among employers.

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Systems analysis
2. Critical thinking
3. Communication
4. Active listening
5. Problem solving
6. Decision making
7. Data analysis
8. Technology evaluation

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Computers and electronics, software and programming, mathematics, business and management, customer needs, data analysis, and information systems.

**Median Annual Salary:**
$105,850 nationally in 2025.

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing / much faster than average. Employment is projected to grow 9% from 2024–2034, compared with 3% for all occupations, with approximately 34,200 openings per year.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
People typically enter the field with a bachelor's degree and develop experience through internships, entry-level analyst positions, and technology projects. With experience, they can advance into senior analyst, systems consultant, project manager, IT manager, or other technology leadership positions.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1. CECS 250 → Develop computational, mathematical, data science, and applied AI skills.
2. AI 101 → Build an understanding of artificial intelligence and its applications.
3. Technology-related internship → Develop real-world systems analysis, communication, and problem-solving skills.
